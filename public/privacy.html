Privacy

What SeerrSense does with your data

Last updated 11 September 2026

SeerrSense sits between an AI assistant and your own Overseerr or Jellyseerr. It holds as little as it can, and this page says plainly what that is. When it changes, the date above moves and the history is public in the repository.

What is stored

DataWhyKept until
Your Google account identifier and email address To know whose request this is and whether you are allowed to use the server You disconnect, or your tokens expire
The address and API key of the Seerr you attached To search and request on your instance rather than a shared one You press Disconnect
Refresh tokens, stored as a hash To keep an assistant connected without asking you to sign in daily 30 days, or until revoked
Pending sign-in state: the handoff between Google, this server and the client To carry one login from Google back to the client that started it 10 minutes
A daily count of language-model lookups, per account To cap what one account can spend on the model each day 7 days

Your Seerr API key is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is written down, and it is never returned by the interface, not even partially masked. No password of yours is ever seen by this service: signing in happens on Google's own pages.

What is not stored

  • Your media library, your watch history, and the requests you have made in Seerr. Those live in your Seerr and are read on demand, never copied here.
  • The content of your conversations with an assistant.
  • Payment details. There are none.

Where data goes

  • To your Seerr. Searches and requests are sent to the instance you attached, with the key you gave, from this server.
  • To Google. Only the sign-in itself. Google tells this service your account identifier, your email address and whether it is verified.
  • To a language model, when one is configured. If the ordinary searches fail to find what you meant, the phrase you typed is sent to a model provider to propose titles. On this server that provider is Nebius (Nebius Token Factory), running an open-weights Qwen model. Only that phrase is sent, never your library, your account or your key, and the proposals are checked back against your Seerr before you see them.
  • Nowhere else. Nothing is sold, shared with advertisers, or used to train models.

What the operator can see

This is a self-operated service, not an anonymous one, and it is honest to say what that means. The person running this server administers the database and holds the key that encrypts stored credentials, so they are technically able to read what is stored, including your Seerr API key. They do not do so as a matter of course, and nothing here is designed to make it convenient, but the encryption protects the data at rest and from anyone else, not from the operator. If that is not an acceptable arrangement for you, run your own copy: the server is open source.

Removing your data

  • To remove everything at once, open your account page and press Delete my account. The attached Seerr and its key, every assistant's sign-in, and the usage counters are deleted immediately, in one step, and nothing is kept in a backup queue afterwards. One thing remains for up to eight hours: a note that your account identifier was deleted at that moment, which is what keeps any browser still signed in on another device from acting on the account. It carries nothing else and expires on its own. No e-mail is needed.
  • To keep the account but detach the Seerr, press Disconnect instead. The stored address and key are deleted immediately.
  • Remove the connector in your assistant as well, which stops it holding tokens for this server, and revoke this application's access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • If anything about that does not work, write to the address below and it will be done by hand.
